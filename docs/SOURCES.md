# The sources: what the house holds from others, and on whose terms

20 November 2026, issue #170. A draft for Will: nothing here is the house's word until he confirms it. Every term below
was read on 20 November 2026 from the source's own published page, linked in its row; a term that could not be read from
here says **not read**, and nothing is filled from memory. Where a source's terms do not settle a question, the row says
**unknown** and the question is in `docs/COUNSEL.md` ("The sources"). We say what we built and what we are unsure of; this
is not legal advice. Nothing here was fetched from an auction house.

Will, 20 Nov: "everything that we brought in is done in the most above-board way. In a way that they would feel honored.
Like their catalogs have been put in a way that they would hire someone to do." The opposite case is on the record: the
Magnus app (2016), other people's data and prices used without their say, pulled from the App Store.

**Counts.** The live house cannot be asked from the workers' shell (the network allow-list refuses watermark.krafftco.com).
What is known: the scale gate's model says museums hold most of the house, "60% and 25%, as the Met and the Gallery do on
live" (`scripts/big-house.js`, 13 Nov); STABILITY.md: "the house grew from 400 works to 20,000 in a week (the Met, MoMA,
the Gallery)"; the Gallery's index held ~59,000 open works (HISTORY, 6 Nov); MoMA's records ~160,000 works, ~16,000
artists (moma.js); Van Gogh's page 957 works (HISTORY, 2 Nov). The live counts, read-only, for the CTO session, are in the
issue (#170, part A).

**Verdicts.** **holds**: how we take it and what we do with it is inside their published terms and their asks.
**fault**: a departure, with the page and the line. **unknown**: their terms do not say; a question for counsel.
**not read**: their page could not be fetched from here.

What a registrar would object to, as the page reads today (each with its place in the code):

1. **The house's number is called "Accession"** and stands at the head of the entry ("WM.2026.0002"), while their accession
   number is inside the catalogue note (`src/views.js` work(), `lot-no` and `facts` 'Accession'). Theirs should lead; the
   house's is the house's register number.
2. **"The public domain attested on arrival to having made this painting and holding its copyright, theirs from the moment
   it was made. The deed."** The Shield's authorship line on every open museum work (the shield block, `shieldBlock`;
   the same words for the Art Institute's desk). False of a museum's photograph of a Rembrandt.
3. **"The maker says never: Training or fine-tuning AI models · Altering the photograph beyond cropping and resizing"** on a
   CC0 work: restrictions the museum never set (the house's default rules on the shelf's maker). The Met's FAQ says
   "Manipulation … is permitted".
4. **"Photographed by The public domain"** (`src/art.js` label) and "Held by Watermark for The public domain".
5. **A thank-you card "to The: 99¢"** and the guest book "You are a guest in The Art Institute of Chicago's studio … The reads
   every line" (`src/views.js` work(): `maker.name.split(' ')[0]`). Money and words addressed to a museum, or to "The".
6. **Medium twice and a camera's medium**: "Oil on canvas" in the object line, then "Digital photograph, 3,000 × 2,400
   pixels" as the medium of the entry (`lot-md`).
7. **"Taken December 31, 1883"** for a work dated 1884 (`taken_at` 1884-01-01 shown in New York time), in "Provenance and record".
8. **Backgrounds "Fill"** crops their picture to a screen (`src/backgrounds.js`). Allowed by CC0; still a cropped copy of
   their photograph under their name.
9. "Dedicated to the public domain (CC0) by its holder" (`office` line) and "The maker has given it to the world" (the open
   block, `src/open.js` OPEN.cc0.says) read as if the painter had made a dedication.
10. The source line names them as a source, never a partner (holds after 1.87.16); the demonstration desk's name, "A museum
   desk (demonstration)", built "on The Metropolitan Museum of Art's Open Access objects", could be read as the Met's desk
   if shown to anyone but the Met (`src/iiif.js` demoDesk).
